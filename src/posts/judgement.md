You're the boss of a company at $10B evaluation, and you have to hire a manager: one is right 70% of the time, and another who is right 71% of the time. Who would you choose? Obviously the one who is right 71% of the time. A person with even just 1% more can save you $100M.

I first learned about this skill called judgement in "The Almanack of Naval Ravikant". The book defines the concept very simply. Wisdom is knowing the consequences of your actions in the long term. Applying wisdom to external problems is judgement.

Current LLMs have drastically reduced the cost of implementing solutions. In the past, if you wanted to build a software, you'd have to learn it, manually type up the code, and debug it if anything is wrong. Today, you can just ask an AI model and it will come up with a working software 
Why judgement?
- Cost of implementing is decreasing.
- An abundance of options

Today's society minimizes the cost of failure, which in turns heavily rewards success. You have an abundant of options available to you nowadays. There are around 8 billion people on the planet.
