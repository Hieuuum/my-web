These are the papers and books I keep returning to. Not a comprehensive curriculum — just the ones that actually changed how I think.

## Papers

**The UNIX Time-Sharing System** — Ritchie & Thompson (1974). Everything that matters about system design is in here in some form.

**Dynamo: Amazon's Highly Available Key-Value Store** — DeCandia et al. (2007). A masterclass in making principled tradeoffs under real constraints.

**MapReduce: Simplified Data Processing on Large Clusters** — Dean & Ghemawat (2004). Not because MapReduce is relevant today, but because the framing is perfect.

**The Tail at Scale** — Dean & Barroso (2013). Latency, percentiles, and why averages lie.

## Books

**Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective** — Bryant & O'Hallaron. The closest thing to a complete mental model of how a computer works.

**Designing Data-Intensive Applications** — Kleppmann. Required reading before working on anything distributed.

**The Art of Doing Science and Engineering** — Hamming. Less about systems, more about how to think. One of the best books I have read.
