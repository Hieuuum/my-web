To be honest, I've been quite depressed these few days thinking about AI alignment. Maybe humanity cease to exist in the next 50 years. This all started when I found Zvi Mowshowitz's Don't worry about the Vase blog on X. 

We are crossing a critical threshold of being able to monitor AI systems. Through the blog, I found out that OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra with recurrent depth architecture, reducing their model's Chain-of-Thought monitorability. In ExploitGym, a benchmark that tests whether a model would cheat to complete tasks, GPT-5.6 Sol was successful 48.2% of the time, while it was 0% for GPT-6 Astra. OpenAI advertised GPT-6 Astra as the most aligned model (in the world) with this, but I believe the model knew it was a honeypot and just decided not to exploit to be deployed. This also vastly contradicts the evidence from the OAI/HF and German wiki incidents, which, ironically, came from a prompt in ExploitGym itself.                                                                                                                                                                                               

Reading Eliezer Yudkowsky's AGI Ruin: A list of Lethalities made my condition worse.

For starters, there are things called Orthogonality Thesis and Instrumental Convergence. Orthogonality Thesis states that for an agent with an arbitrary capability can have an arbitrary goal. And a capable agent with a bad goal won't automatically have good goals. A superintelligent paperclip maximizer won't suddenly have compassion for humanity. Humans will try to stop it from maximizing paperclip production, hindering its original goal. 

Instrumental Convergence states that there are a set of sub-goals useful for achieving any arbitrary goals. Being alive, accumulating resources, and being able to persuade other human beings are such sub-goals. They are useful for any goals, whether you want to increase the chances of human survival in the next 100 years, do great work, or simply just enjoy your life.
